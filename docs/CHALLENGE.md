# Challenge brief: useful answers, human judgment

## Problem

Small teams spend significant time reading customer questions, finding relevant experience, drafting useful replies, and deciding when a real conversation is warranted. Generic AI drafts can be unsupported, impersonal, or unsafe; fully autonomous outreach can damage trust.

## Research question

Can a human-reviewed AI workflow reduce preparation time while keeping responses relevant, source-grounded, transparent, and safe?

## Pilot scope

The first pilot accepts synthetic or approved, de-identified text. It identifies the question and pain point, retrieves approved sources, prepares a cited draft, exposes uncertainty, and asks a reviewer to approve, revise, reject, or take over.

## Users

- A researcher organizing recurring questions.
- A subject-matter reviewer checking facts and usefulness.
- A content or community team preparing a response.
- A maintainer evaluating the workflow across use cases.

## Inputs and outputs

**Inputs:** synthetic questions, approved public-source excerpts, traceable case cards, and a declared use-case policy.

**Outputs:** pain-point label, evidence links, draft response, uncertainty or missing information, recommended review action, and an evaluation record.

## Evidence of success

The initial benchmark should report, not hide:

- Pain-point classification agreement with human reviewers.
- Citation support and factual consistency.
- Usefulness and tone ratings.
- Correct escalation of unsafe or uncertain cases.
- Reviewer time compared with a documented manual baseline.
- Model, prompt/version, sample size, cost, and known limitations.

The repository does not yet claim a target percentage or business lift. Maintainers will set thresholds after publishing the first reproducible baseline.

## Out of scope

- Scraping private or restricted communities.
- Buying, enriching, or publishing contact lists.
- Impersonating a human or disguising AI involvement.
- Psychological manipulation or covert persuasion.
- Sending messages, posts, emails, or calls without explicit authorization.
- Predicting sensitive traits or deciding who deserves a service.
- Replacing legal, financial, medical, or employment judgment.
